AI Decision Support Training

You will see an AI risk score (probability) that estimates the chance of reintubation within 48 hours if the patient is extubated now. No threshold marker is shown.

Probability output 0–100% Decision support

Key idea: The AI provides a continuous risk signal (e.g., 12% vs 62%). You decide how to use it along with clinical judgment.

1) What the AI uses

The AI reads only the information shown in the survey case table (examples: age, vitals, ventilation duration, labs, qSOFA). It was trained on historical ICU cases labeled by whether 48-hour reintubation occurred.

Think of it as: Inputs → ML model → risk probability for the 48-hour outcome.
Important limitation: If something is not in the table (e.g., secretion burden, sedation plan, trajectory, imaging, neuro status), the AI cannot account for it.

Diagram: patient inputs → ML → risk score (0–100%).

2) What the probability means

If the AI shows Risk = 20%, it means: among similar patients (based on the displayed inputs, and similar to the training data), about 20 out of 100 would be expected to require reintubation within 48 hours if extubated now.
What it does NOT mean:
  • Not a guarantee. 20% does not mean this patient “will fail.”
  • Not causality. The model predicts risk; it does not prove extubation causes reintubation.
  • Not complete. Risk is based only on the inputs shown, not the full clinical picture.

Quick interpretation guide

Risk levelHow to use it
Low (example: 0–15%) AI suggests low concern based on recorded inputs. Still verify stability and readiness.
Intermediate (example: 15–35%) Uncertainty is often higher. Re-check missing clinical factors and consider additional evidence.
High (example: >35%) AI suggests higher concern. Consider whether risks are modifiable or whether more time/support is needed.

These ranges are examples for intuition; the survey does not provide a threshold.

Interpret the number as a continuous signal (higher = more concern), not as certainty.

3) Recommended workflow (30 seconds)

  1. Review the case table first (vitals, trajectory, ventilation duration, labs, etc.).
  2. Look at the AI risk: does it match your clinical impression?
  3. If mismatch, ask: “What important factors are missing from the inputs?” (secretions, sedation, neuro status, imaging, trend).
Reminder: This is decision support. Final responsibility remains with the clinician.
