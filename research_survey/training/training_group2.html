AI Decision Support Training — Group 2

In Group 2, you will see an AI risk score (probability) and a threshold line. The threshold is used to convert the probability into an AI “yes/no” recommendation internally.

Probability output Threshold shown Decision support

Key idea: Compare AI risk to the threshold. If risk is above the threshold, the AI would lean “higher risk.” You still decide what to do clinically.

1) What the AI uses

The AI reads only the information shown in the survey case table (examples: age, vitals, ventilation duration, labs, qSOFA). It was trained on historical ICU cases labeled by whether 48-hour reintubation occurred.

Think of it as: Inputs → ML model → risk probability for the 48-hour outcome.
Important limitation: If something is not in the table (e.g., secretion burden, sedation plan, trajectory, imaging, neuro status), the AI cannot account for it.

Diagram: patient inputs → ML → risk score (0–100%) with threshold marker.

2) What the probability means

If the AI shows Risk = 20%, it means: among similar patients (based on the displayed inputs, and similar to the training data), about 20 out of 100 would be expected to require reintubation within 48 hours if extubated now.
What it does NOT mean:
  • Not a guarantee. 20% does not mean this patient “will fail.”
  • Not causality. The model predicts risk; it does not prove extubation causes reintubation.
  • Not complete. Risk is based only on the inputs shown, not the full clinical picture.

3) What the threshold means

A threshold is a chosen cut-point (example: 42%). It is used to translate a probability into a simple recommendation. Thresholds reflect a trade-off between sensitivity and specificity, and may be selected to fit a clinical goal.

How to read it:
  • If Risk ≥ Threshold → AI leans toward “higher risk” (more concern for 48h reintubation).
  • If Risk < Threshold → AI leans toward “lower risk” (less concern).

Example (for understanding the display)

0 = low risk  •  100 = high risk
Threshold 42%
AI 62%
If the AI score is above the threshold, the AI’s internal “yes/no” would likely be “higher risk.” You still use clinical judgment.
Important: A threshold is not “the correct answer.” It is a policy choice. Two clinicians can reasonably act differently at the same risk level.

4) Recommended workflow (30 seconds)

  1. Review the case table first (vitals, trajectory, ventilation duration, labs, etc.).
  2. Look at AI risk and threshold: is the score above or below the threshold?
  3. If mismatch with your impression, ask: “What factors are missing from the inputs?” (secretions, sedation, neuro, imaging, trend).
Reminder: This is decision support. Final responsibility remains with the clinician.

5) Mini glossary

TermMeaning
